Museum Catalogue Entry

Impression, Sunrise

Claude Monet

Date
1872
Movement
Impressionism
Medium
Oil on canvas
Dimensions
50 × 65 cm

The painting that named a movement

A fleeting dawn over Le Havre became a manifesto for modern vision: light, atmosphere, and immediate perception replace polished academic description.

Historical Context

Monet painted the outer harbour of Le Havre from the Hôtel de l’Amirauté around November 1872. When shown at the independent group’s 1874 exhibition, its title helped critic Louis Leroy coin the label “Impressionists.”

Composition

Three dark boats anchor the foreground. Masts, cranes, smokestacks, and quays dissolve into mist, while the orange sun and its broken reflection hold the scene together.

Colour and Light

Blue-grey vapour fills the harbour. Against it, the complementary orange sun appears unusually intense. The contrast communicates light without conventional modelling.

Brushwork

Thin, rapid strokes preserve the speed of observation. Monet allows separate marks to remain visible, asking the viewer’s eye to assemble water, boats, smoke, and distance.

Modernity

This is not an untouched natural landscape. Industry, transport, mist, and morning labour share the harbour, making the painting an image of modern life as well as atmosphere.

Look Closely

  • Follow the interrupted orange reflection.
  • Compare solid boats with the dissolving background.
  • Notice how little detail is needed to suggest a working port.

Timeline

  1. 1872 Monet paints the harbour at dawn.
  2. 1874 The work appears in the first Impressionist exhibition.
  3. 1940 It enters the Musée Marmottan collection through the Donop de Monchy bequest.
  4. Today It remains a defining work of Impressionism.
