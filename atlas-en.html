Practical answers

Frequently asked questions

Useful information about the book, accessing experiences and the collection.

What is ARTDACI?

ARTDACI is a printed art book and an independent website for discovering paintings and art history through connected knowledge and selected AR, 3D and VR experiences.

Do I need the printed book?

No. You can explore the website and many of its digital experiences without owning the book. The printed book offers an additional reading and discovery journey.

What equipment do I need?

A modern browser is sufficient for many pages. Camera-based AR requires a compatible device and camera permission. Some immersive VR features may require suitable hardware.

How much does it cost?

You can explore the public website without buying the printed book. Any book purchase terms or experience-specific charges must be confirmed separately.

Which artworks are included?

ARTDACI features works by Leonardo da Vinci, Johannes Vermeer, Vincent van Gogh and Claude Monet. Visit the Painting Index to discover the collection and individual experiences.

Explore the Augmented Atlas concept

Paper First, Magic Second

Every spread should remain a complete editorial experience on its own: context, analysis, technique, symbolism, chronology, details to observe, and links to other works. The augmented layer is an enrichment, never a requirement. A reader who scans nothing should still receive a rich and coherent experience.

Book structure

The Atlas organizes masterpieces into a clear museum-style journey that connects artists, periods, techniques, and visual ideas.

  • Artist and artwork introductions
  • Historical and artistic context
  • Technique, composition, palette, and symbolism
  • Timelines, comparisons, and close-looking prompts
  • Connections to museums and immersive experiences

Spread model

The artwork remains visually central, while the facing content provides the information needed to understand and observe it carefully.

  • Title, artist, date, medium, dimensions, and museum
  • Historical context and artistic movement
  • Composition, palette, technique, and symbolism
  • Hidden details, anecdotes, restoration, and influence
  • Timeline and comparisons with other works

Augmented modes

Image ARThe printed artwork becomes a target for 3D models, labels, audio, video, and contextual layers.
NarrationShort audio guides provide an accessible introduction without replacing the printed reading experience.
Interactive labelsConcise hotspots reveal composition, technique, symbolism, and hidden details.
3D reconstructionModels can emerge from the page or be placed independently in the reader’s physical space.
Artistic processFuture layers may reveal underdrawings, glazing, restoration, X-rays, or other technical imagery.
Museum and timeSelected works can extend into galleries, studios, cities, or reconstructed museum spaces.

Personal gallery

Beyond page scanning, spatial mode can let readers place 3D content in their own environment, compare works, enlarge details, and build a personal gallery around the printed book.

QR strategy

QR codes complement rather than replace image tracking. They should remain discreet and provide direct access to longer videos, learning resources, spatial galleries, language options, and evolving online content.

Education

  • Observation questions for younger readers, students, and adults
  • Guided comparisons between works, periods, and techniques
  • Symbol and hidden-detail exploration
  • Teacher resources and discussion prompts

Accessibility

  • Audio descriptions and spoken overviews
  • Subtitles for narration and video
  • Readable typography and strong contrast
  • Short navigation paths back to the printed-book experience

Development path

  1. Print. A complete museum-style book that works independently.
  2. Augment. Image tracking, narration, labels, 3D, video, and QR access.
  3. Immerse. Spatial galleries, WebXR, VR, reconstructed places, and richer interactions.
  4. Extend. Future glasses and spatial-computing experiences with hands-free interpretation and museum guidance.
